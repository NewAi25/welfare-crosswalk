# Kevin's brief: item #13 and the question

Source: Kevin Xia's Top 30 project list, https://docs.google.com/document/d/19NuA2feGXX-PNUEVo15jUZZWd6AaH95bSDqAbO1WUEg/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.3o23qbiu5ocw. Text pasted by Manisha Sarkar on 25 September 2026.

## The question the smaller scope answers

"Which welfare indicators are (a) measurable by AI, (b) acceptable to industry for adoption, and (c) potentially already covered by existing sensors or widely-used certification schemes?"

## Item #13, verbatim

> 13. Welfare indicators × deployed sensors × certification requirements
> Factory Farming | ~25h | [◆]
> Three-way crosswalk for one species: validated welfare indicators (Welfare Quality, AWIN, species literature) against what commercial PLF sensors actually measure against what certification schemes require and audit. The valuable cells are indicators already instrumented but not required, and indicators required but manually audited.
> Non-obvious: the size of the already-instrumented-but-not-required set is unknown and could be large, in which case there is a cheap ask sitting there. Knowable: yes. Unoccupied: each column exists separately; the crosswalk doesn't.
> Decomposition: one species per unit.

## How this repository reads it

Column 1 for dairy comes from the Welfare Quality dairy protocol and the EFSA 2023 dairy cow opinion; AWIN has no cattle protocol, so it does not apply to the dairy unit and is noted for the poultry unit only where it has one (AWIN covers turkeys, not broilers or laying hens). The two valuable cells are kept as named views of the adoption table. Kevin's Slack reply of 25 September (docs/kevin_answers.md) turns criteria (a), (b) and (c) into soft adoption factors, and the deliverable into a ranked table rather than a filtered list.
