# 13. Welfare indicators × deployed sensors × certification requirements: dairy cows

Factory Farming | Dairy unit done, poultry is future scope | [◆]

This entry follows the format of the Top 30 project list by Kevin Xia, the project's mentor; the marker [◆] is his. A crosswalk is a table that lines up separate lists side by side.

A three way crosswalk for dairy cows: 81 rows of welfare indicators and sensor parameters, against the 129 sensor products in the 2021 review by Stygar and colleagues and studies since, against what four certification schemes require and how they check it. The rows are grouped into 52 welfare states, the condition or problem each row measures, and 46 are ranked on a priority list by how well the best sensor test did, then the device needed, then how hard the measure is to take by hand. Each state also gets the kind of ask it supports. Six rows the welfare sources do not back leave the list and are reported on their own.

Result: the sensors that do well are not measuring what schemes ask for. On the ranked list, the five states whose sensors passed the high performance bar are not known to be required by any scheme, and rank first on the list. The four states schemes require, with a sensor on offer, have weaker evidence: one test cannot be graded, two fell short and one is only claimed. No scheme's numbered standards accept sensor data as the way an inspector checks any row.

The two valuable cells, the ones Kevin's list names. Already instrumented but not required: 21 rows, 17 of them still on the priority list, and 12 more unclear. Required but manually audited: 24 rows.

Non-obvious: the ask may be cheapest away from the schemes, as the summary Kevin forwarded suggests, though the table holds no cost data. For four states, products collect the signal and no scheme requires it, but no description in the 2021 product list says it is for health or welfare, and three of those passed the bar: feeding time, feed intake and rumination. On farms that own the devices, new software could turn them to welfare without new hardware. The ask that looks most natural, getting schemes to accept sensor data for what they already require, is not yet supported by the tests. Lameness is required by all four schemes and checked by hand under all four, but its sensors fell short of the bar.

Recommended next: welfare alerts on signals products already collect; schemes adopting lying time; independent validation before asking schemes to accept sensor data; schemes clarifying the wording that leaves eight states unclear; new measurement for the 20 states where no product is graded as measuring the state.

Knowable: yes, from public documents only. Every value names its source and follows a written rule, and 54 questions the rules could not settle are published as open. The list does not say how much each state matters to the cow, because no source used here ranks the states across the table.

Unoccupied: each column existed separately; the crosswalk did not.

Decomposition: one species per unit. Poultry can reuse the same tables and rules.

Key. The World labels come from a summary Kevin forwarded with his review. World A: accept sensor data means a scheme requires part of the state and a product measures or claims to measure part of it. World A: add to schemes means a product's description says the sensor is for health or welfare but no scheme requires the state. World B: new measurement needed means no product is graded as measuring the state. World C: add welfare alerts means at least one product collects the signal and no scheme requires it, but no description in the 2021 product list says it is for health or welfare. Unclear means the written rules do not settle it. The high performance bar is the one Stygar and colleagues use: every result a study reports must meet it, for example a correlation, how closely two sets of readings move together, above 0.9. Claimed means a product says it measures this and no test that counts was found. Instrumented means a product measures it or claims to. A scheme is a certification scheme, the standards behind a welfare label. Non-obvious, Knowable, Unoccupied and Decomposition are the headings of Kevin's list: what is surprising, whether it can be found out, whether anyone else has done it, and how it divides.
