# Priority table, dairy cows

The short answer: the welfare states in the order of the ranking, and the kind of ask each one supports. This page is written by a script from the research tables and is never edited by hand. The full evidence, row by row with its sources, is in [welfare_crosswalk_dairy.xlsx](welfare_crosswalk_dairy.xlsx). The method and recommendations are in [write_up.md](write_up.md), and the rules are in [rules.md](../2_research/rules.md), section 5.

The ranking is a stated sort order. It is not a welfare judgement, and it does not say how much each state matters to the cow: EFSA does not rank its welfare consequences, and Welfare Quality orders its criteria only within each of its principles.

## How to read the columns

**Best test** is how the best sensor test for the state did. The bar is the one Stygar and colleagues 2021 use for high performance, and every result a study reports must meet it. Cannot be graded yet means the results available do not settle it. **Device needed** is the cheapest kind of equipment for the best indicator. **Hard to take by hand?** reads EFSA's rating of how feasible the measure is on a farm the other way round, because a measure that is hard to take by hand is where a sensor adds the most; Not rated means EFSA does not rate it; such rows come after rated ones when this column breaks a tie. **Kind of ask** is what the state supports. A, accept: a scheme requires the state and a sensor exists. A, adopt: products use the sensor for health or welfare but no scheme requires it. B: no product is graded as measuring the state. C: products collect the signal and no scheme requires it, but no description in the 2021 product list says it is for health or welfare. **Flag** names rows whose link to welfare a source questions, or which the rules leave unclear. The Priority sheet of the Excel file gives more columns.
