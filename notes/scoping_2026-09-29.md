# Scoping check: has anyone updated Stygar 2021?

Run on 29 September 2026 after Manisha asked why column 2 reads from two papers only. The question for this check: does a review exist, published since 2021, that does for dairy sensors what Stygar et al. did, so that the table can be brought up to date without an open ended search? Nothing in this note changes any row; it informs a decision.

## How the check was run

Three routes. First, every paper that cites Stygar 2021 in Europe PMC (98 papers), filtered for titles naming a review, a survey, a validation or a commercial system together with a dairy or cattle term, which left 22. Second, a Europe PMC title search for reviews from 2021 to 2026 combining a cattle term with a sensor term, 28 hits, and a title search for primary validation studies over the same years, 22 hits. Third, one web search for a systematic review of validated commercial dairy sensors from 2023 onward. Each candidate below was then confirmed on Crossref by DOI for authors, journal, volume and licence, and its abstract read in Europe PMC. No full text has been read yet, so what each paper contains beyond its abstract is not yet known.

## Finding

Nobody has repeated Stygar's method, which was every sensor type, commercial availability from a market search, and external validation from a literature search, in one table. There is no drop in replacement. There are partial updates, all open access under CC BY 4.0.

| Paper | What the abstract says it covers | Bears on |
|---|---|---|
| Lee, Brause, Foy and Cantor 2025, Animal 19:101613, doi 10.1016/j.animal.2025.101613 | A scoping review of 101 original articles from the past 11 years that validated wearable sensors for dairy cattle behaviour, from 2,955 search results in PubMed and Web of Science. Activity was studied in 61 of the 101, consumption in 59, resting in 55, digestive behaviours in 49. 94 met its reproducibility criteria, 40 calculated precision or a proxy, and 14 of the 101 met all of its validity criteria | Column 2, the wearable behaviour rows: lying, steps, feeding, rumination |
| Hudson, Nicodemus, McGee, McKnight and Harvey 2026, Animals 16(17):2643, doi 10.3390/ani16172643 | A review of recent validation studies of automated lameness detection: sensor, pressure, vision and multimodal systems, reported accuracies from about 70% to 98%, with a risk of bias assessment. Studies were screened by a single reviewer | Column 2, lameness (I001) and the camera rows |
| Lamanna, Bovo and Cavallini 2025, Animals 15(3):458, doi 10.3390/ani15030458 | A systematized review of the sensors built into collars and their functions. The abstract is about function and design, not validation | Column 2, hardware class and what collars measure |
| Linstädt, Thöne-Reineke and Merle 2024, Frontiers in Veterinary Science 11:1429097, doi 10.3389/fvets.2024.1429097 | A systematic review of animal based welfare indicators for dairy cows and their validity and practicality, literature from 2011 to 2021, five databases | Column 1 |
| Debertolis, Holighaus, Gauly and Zanon 2026, Veterinary and Animal Science 31:100587, doi 10.1016/j.vas.2026.100587 | A modified systematic review of compliance frameworks for dairy cattle welfare in Europe, with a qualitative comparison of nine frameworks in France, Italy, Germany, Austria and Switzerland | Column 3, and the claim in Kevin's brief that the crosswalk is unoccupied |

Two cautions. Lee and colleagues report that 14 of 101 studies met their validity criteria, and Stygar report that 18 of 129 products were externally validated; both come to 14%, but the first counts studies against a statistical standard and the second counts products against an independence standard, so the two figures must not be presented as the same finding. And the Debertolis review covers Alpine frameworks, not the four schemes in this crosswalk, and its abstract does not mention sensors; it should be read before the write up repeats that the crosswalk is unoccupied.

## What it means for the decision

An update to column 2 can be read mostly from published reviews, not built from an open search. The design that keeps the table auditable is a second, separate evidence column: the Stygar grade stays as the 2021 baseline, and a new column records evidence since 2021 with its source, so a reader can see which is which. Estimated cost, with the full texts still unread: Lee 2025 for the wearable rows, about three hours; Hudson 2026 for lameness, about one hour; a bounded search with a written protocol for the milk and metabolic rows, which no review found here covers, about three hours. Six to eight hours in total, against eight to twelve for a search from nothing.

What this check did not do: read any full text, search Scopus or Web of Science, which are not open, or look at vendor pages. The title searches are narrow by construction, so the counts of primary studies are floors.

## Second check, the same day: Maroto Molina 2020

Manisha asked the same question of the second source. Maroto Molina et al. 2020 is a Research Reflection of six pages in the Journal of Dairy Research, a considered opinion piece, not a systematic review. Europe PMC records 15 papers citing it, against 97 for Stygar. It is the only source behind every research_only and none grade in column 2, and several of the technologies it proposes come from other species or settings (pigs, carcasses, human skin), which the rows already say.

The check: the 15 papers citing it, and a Europe PMC title search for 2020 to 2026 combining a welfare assessment term, a sensor or automation term and a cattle term, 7 hits. Each candidate confirmed on Crossref; abstracts only, no full text read.

| Paper | What the abstract says it covers | Bears on |
|---|---|---|
| Leliveld and Provolo 2020, Animals 10(8):1430, doi 10.3390/ani10081430, CC BY | A review built on 4 reviews of cow welfare in general and 48 reviews of single welfare issues, identifying 18 major welfare issues and 76 matching indicators that could be detected automatically on the farm | The nearest parallel to Maroto Molina, from the same year and on a wider base. A second source for the research_only and none grades |
| Silva, Araujo, Guedes, Silva, Almeida and Cerqueira 2021, Animals 11(8):2253, doi 10.3390/ani11082253, CC BY | Precision technologies for dairy cattle welfare, focused on lameness, mastitis and body condition | Rows I001, I006, I023, I057 |
| Aubé, Mialon, Mollaret, Mounier, Veissier and de Boyer des Roches 2022, Animal 16(8):100597, doi 10.1016/j.animal.2022.100597, CC BY | Assessment of dairy cow welfare at pasture: measures available and gaps | The pasture and outdoor access rows |
| Islam, Edwards, Burns, Qi and Gan 2026, Sensors 26(13):4271, doi 10.3390/s26134271, CC BY | A review of computer vision for cattle health and welfare monitoring | Every row where Maroto Molina propose a camera |
| Fuentes, Han, Liu, Park, Yoon and Park 2026, Animal 20(4):101788, doi 10.1016/j.animal.2026.101788, CC BY NC ND | A review of AI technology for cattle social behaviour in penned systems | Agonistic interactions and the grooming rows |
| Tuyttens, de Graaf, Andreasen, de Boyer des Roches, van Eerdenburg, Haskell and others 2021, Frontiers in Veterinary Science 8:634470, doi 10.3389/fvets.2021.634470, CC BY | Expert elicitation to abridge the Welfare Quality protocol to the most adverse dairy welfare impairments | Column 1: which Welfare Quality measures experts rank as mattering most |

Finding: nobody has repeated Maroto Molina's measure by measure mapping of the Welfare Quality protocol either. Leliveld and Provolo is the nearest and rests on far more literature. The two reviews from 2026, on computer vision and on social behaviour, cover exactly the rows where Maroto Molina could only say that a camera might be adapted.

What it means: the research_only and none grades are the weakest part of column 2, because they rest on one group's reflection from 2019. Two steps would strengthen them. A cross check of every research_only and none row against Leliveld and Provolo, about two hours, which either confirms the grade or shows a disagreement to record. And the two 2026 reviews added to the evidence since 2021 column, about two to three hours. With these the update pass is nine to eleven hours, not six to eight.
