## 2026-09-29: correction, which trait is validated most often

The entry of 25 September called rumination the trait Stygar validate most often. That is wrong. Stygar page 5 to 6: non active behavior (lying, lying and standing, and standing) as well as rumination and feeding time were the most often validated attributes, with 20, 15 and 11 trials respectively. Lying and standing is first, rumination second, feeding time third. By sensor type, page 5: accelerometer based systems have the highest validation rate, 30% of tools on the market, against cameras 10%, load cells 8%, milk sensors 8% and boluses 7%, and, on page 4, 26 of the validation trials were on accelerometers. The observation that stands: rumination, with 15 trials, maps to no EFSA or Welfare Quality measure, and non active behaviour (lying, lying and standing, and standing), the most validated, maps only to lying time, an EFSA measure with no Welfare Quality equivalent. The first audit confirmed only that rumination maps to no indicator and did not check the ranking claim; found when Manisha asked which was most validated. To be re-verified by the auditor with batch 2.

## 2026-09-29: why column 2 reads from two papers, and the scoping check

Manisha asked who decided that column 2 reads only from Stygar 2021 and Maroto Molina 2020. The answer recorded here: the restriction was a design choice made when the definitions were written, not an instruction from Kevin, and it was accepted without being examined. Its reasons are that Stygar apply a stated standard for validation, that every grade can be checked against a page, and that the scope is 25 hours. Its cost is that the table describes the market and the literature as they stood around 2020: later validation studies and later products are invisible, and a grade of none means none in those two papers.

A scoping check on 29 September (notes/scoping_2026-09-29.md) found no review that repeats Stygar's method, and several partial updates, chiefly Lee, Brause, Foy and Cantor 2025 on wearable behaviour sensors and Hudson et al. 2026 on automated lameness detection. Pending Manisha's decision, the proposal is to keep the Stygar grade as the baseline and add a separate column for evidence since 2021, read from those reviews and from a bounded, written search for the rows they do not cover. No row has been changed.
