## 2026-09-29: correction, which trait is validated most often

The entry of 25 September called rumination the trait Stygar validate most often. That is wrong. Stygar page 5 to 6: non active behavior (lying, lying and standing, and standing) as well as rumination and feeding time were the most often validated attributes, with 20, 15 and 11 trials respectively. Lying and standing is first, rumination second, feeding time third. By sensor type, page 5: accelerometer based systems have the highest validation rate, 30% of tools on the market, against cameras 10%, load cells 8%, milk sensors 8% and boluses 7%, and, on page 4, 26 of the validation trials were on accelerometers. The observation that stands: rumination, with 15 trials, maps to no EFSA or Welfare Quality measure, and non active behaviour (lying, lying and standing, and standing), the most validated, maps only to lying time, an EFSA measure with no Welfare Quality equivalent. The first audit confirmed only that rumination maps to no indicator and did not check the ranking claim; found when Manisha asked which was most validated. To be re-verified by the auditor with batch 2.

## 2026-09-29: why column 2 reads from two papers, and the scoping check

Manisha asked who decided that column 2 reads only from Stygar 2021 and Maroto Molina 2020. The answer recorded here: the restriction was a design choice made when the definitions were written, not an instruction from Kevin, and it was accepted without being examined. Its reasons are that Stygar apply a stated standard for validation, that every grade can be checked against a page, and that the scope is 25 hours. Its cost is that the table describes the market and the literature as they stood around 2020: later validation studies and later products are invisible, and a grade of none means none in those two papers.

A scoping check on 29 September (notes/scoping_2026-09-29.md) found no review that repeats Stygar's method, and several partial updates, chiefly Lee, Brause, Foy and Cantor 2025 on wearable behaviour sensors and Hudson et al. 2026 on automated lameness detection. Pending Manisha's decision, the proposal is to keep the Stygar grade as the baseline and add a separate column for evidence since 2021, read from those reviews and from a bounded, written search for the rows they do not cover. No row has been changed.

## 2026-10-08: rules for reading the certification schemes, and EFSA's own ratings added

Four readers, one per scheme, read the whole of RSPCA Assured dairy cattle 2026, FARM Animal Care v5, Global Animal Partnership dairy v2.0 and Certified Humane Edition 23 against all 58 indicators. 1. A new column, requirement_status, says whether the scheme requires the indicator: required, unsure (a candidate requirement exists but the rules do not settle whether it names the measure) or not_required. Only required counts toward the schemes requiring an indicator; an unsure row makes Kevin's lists Unclear for that indicator and has a line in open_questions.md naming the indicator and the scheme. how_checked unsure is kept only for a required row whose method the scheme leaves open. This replaces the earlier use of how_checked unsure for naming that was unsettled, which would have counted those rows as required.
2. Only numbered requirements count, as RSPCA Assured and Certified Humane state of their own standards; guidance in boxes or notes is recorded in reasoning.
3. An appendix, scorecard or protocol counts when a numbered requirement makes it binding (for example RSPCA's Appendix 4 through WA 1.1 a)).
4. A requirement counts only if it applies to every certified farm: not one limited to a housing type, triggered only above a target, in an optional module, or, for Global Animal Partnership, applying only above Step 1.
5. A disease named without its form names only the clinical case or incidence row for that disease (ketosis I020, hypocalcaemia or milk fever I028, displaced abomasum I024, mastitis I006), never a subclinical row or a row defined by a test.
6. A direct synonym names the same measure in the same animals; broader wording, other animals or another level do not count. A herd level or bulk tank somatic cell count names I007, not the individual cow rows I057 and I058.
7. The rule covers the indicator's own measure whatever its measure type, so a requirement that names a resource or management measure names it.
8. visual_inspection covers an assessor observing animals or, for a resource based measure, the resource or equipment.
9. A combined score names each body region its own text lists; a region shown only in a drawing does not count.
10. A housing, equipment or space requirement does not name an animal measure even when it states the outcome it is meant to allow, such as housing that allows cows to lie down easily, following the cubicle example already in rule 4.8.
11. A requirement names a condition's case or incidence measure when it requires the farm to record, monitor or report cases of that named condition; a treatment protocol alone, or treatment records that name no condition, do not.
12. Thermal comfort has no measure in Welfare Quality, so no requirement can name it; requirements on the thermal environment are recorded as related.
13. number_in_requirement keeps any number or frequency the requirement sets, as written, matching the RSPCA worked example.
14. Global Animal Partnership is read at Step 1, the level every certified farm meets; this is a case of rule 4.
